Why a monopoly is inefficient

Because P > MR = MC, the monopolist stops short of the efficient quantity. A competitive market would keep producing until P = MC — a larger quantity at a lower price.

The units between the monopoly quantity and the competitive quantity are worth more to buyers than they cost to make, but never get produced. That lost value is the deadweight loss — the triangle between demand and MC.

Market power, not higher cost, is what raises the price: same cost curves, fewer units, a higher price, and profit that can persist because barriers to entry keep rivals out.
